They ask for a valuation report. What they need is someone to tell them whether the deal makes sense.

They ask for a compliance sign-off. What they need is someone to flag what the regulation doesn't say but clearly intends.

They ask for an audit. What they need is someone to tell them why the numbers feel wrong even when they balance.

In twenty years of sitting across boardroom tables, I've noticed a pattern.

The ask is always technical. The need is always human.

A number. A signature. A report. These are the deliverables boards request because they are the things they know how to ask for.

What they cannot easily ask for is judgment. Clarity. The perspective of someone who has seen this situation before and knows how it ends.

That's not a product. You can't put it on an invoice.

But it's the thing that changes the outcome.

The best board advisors I've respected don't wait to be asked for it. They bring it anyway.
