Years ago, I asked a promoter what his worst case scenario was.

Most promoters answer that question with confidence. "It won't happen." "We have backup plans." "We are diversified."

This promoter answered differently.

He paused. He thought for a minute. Then he listed three specific things that would have to go wrong, in sequence, for his business to fail. He told me which one he could absorb, which one would hurt, and which one would end the company.

For each one, he told me what he had already done to prepare.

I left that meeting changed.

I had been confusing confidence with clarity for years. Most of my clients did the same.

Confidence is what people show when they want to convince others. Clarity is what they have when they have already convinced themselves.

The two look similar from outside. Inside, they are completely different.

Confident people answer fast. They speak in absolutes. They project certainty.

Clear people answer slowly. They acknowledge uncertainty. They distinguish between what they know, what they assume, and what they have not thought through.

In 23 years of work, the businesses that lasted were not run by the most confident promoters. They were run by the ones who could tell me exactly which of their assumptions could break, and what they had built to absorb that break.

Confidence persuades. Clarity protects.

When I sit across from a founder today, I am not listening for confidence. I am listening for clarity.

The two sound very different once you have learned to tell them apart.
