In a recent boardroom, the script was painfully familiar:

- Marketing said: "Production couldn't deliver, we lost the orders."
- Production said: "Finance didn't release advances, raw material never arrived on time."
- Finance said: "Cash flow was tight, we did the best we could."
- The Board concluded: "Market conditions were tough this time."

Everyone defended their corner with conviction. Everyone had data. Everyone was right.

And yet the company missed its numbers. Again.

This is not a department problem. This is not even a people problem. This is a governance blind spot.

Senior teams are engaged. Directors are present in daily operations. MIS reports circulate on time. But somewhere between the noise of activity and the comfort of involvement, no one is asking the uncomfortable question: "Why does the same problem keep arriving every month, just wearing a different costume?"

A board is not a referee blowing the whistle after the goal is scored. At least one voice in the room must be willing to:

- See the pattern, not just the incident.
- Connect cash flow, procurement, production and market as one chain, not four silos.
- Flag risks 60 to 90 days before they hit the P&L.
- Ask the question no one in the operating team is paid to ask.

That is the quiet, often unpopular, but essential job of an Independent Director.

Not to fix every problem. Not to know every answer. But to ensure the right problem is named, early, clearly and honestly, even when the room would rather not hear it.

Because in well-run companies, problems don't disappear. They just get caught upstream.
