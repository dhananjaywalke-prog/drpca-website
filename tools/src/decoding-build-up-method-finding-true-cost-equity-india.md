How do investors decide what return they deserve for taking a risk on a company? At the heart of every business valuation lies one crucial number, the **Cost of Equity**. It represents the return investors expect for putting their money into a business instead of a risk-free asset.

But in India, where reliable market data is limited and private companies dominate, valuers often turn to a pragmatic and transparent tool, the **Build-Up Method**.

## Build-Up Method

The **Build-Up Method** does exactly what its name suggests, it *builds up* the cost of equity step by step by adding various layers of risk.

It typically starts with the **risk-free rate** (like yield on 10-year Government of India bonds), and then adds premiums for:
- **Equity Risk Premium (ERP):** the extra return for investing in equities over risk-free bonds.
- **Size Premium:** smaller companies are riskier, so investors demand more return.
- **Industry or Sector Premium:** sectors like technology or real estate may carry additional volatility.
- **Company-Specific Risk Premium (CSRP):** this reflects unique risks such as customer concentration, management depth, or governance quality.

**Example:** If the 10-year government bond yield is 7%, and total risk premiums add up to 9%, the total **Cost of Equity = 16%**. This “built-up” rate becomes a critical input in **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)** valuations, helping determine the fair value of a business.

## Insights from Valuation Standards

**International Valuation Standards (IVS)** and **ICAI Valuation Standards (VS 103)** both recognize the Build-Up Method as one of the most **professional yet judgment-driven tools for valuers**. However, valuers should consider the following while applying it:
- **Data Sources Matter:** Indian valuers often rely on **RBI bond yields**, **Bloomberg data**, and **Duff & Phelps ERP studies**. The chosen benchmarks must be relevant to the valuation date.
- **Size Premium in Indian Context:** ICAI guidance notes highlight that smaller, unlisted companies, typical in India’s SME and startup ecosystem, deserve a higher size premium due to limited access to capital and liquidity.
- **Professional Judgment is Key:** IVS and ICAI both warn against arbitrary premiums. Each component must be justified, ideally by past performance, peer data, or sectoral benchmarks.
- **Integration with DCF Models:** The Build-Up rate directly affects discount rates, influencing valuation outcomes significantly. Even a 1% change in cost of equity can shift valuation by **5–10%**.

Together, these insights reinforce that the **Build-Up Method** is not just a calculation, it’s a *story of risk translated into numbers*.

**In India’s dynamic market, the Build-Up Method remains one of the most adaptable and transparent ways to estimate the cost of equity, especially for unlisted or privately held firms.**

It reminds us that valuation is not about perfection, but about *reasoned judgment backed by consistent methodology*. When done right, it bridges the gap between market perception and professional analysis, helping investors and businesses make informed, defensible decisions.
