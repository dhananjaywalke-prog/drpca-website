It was 1991. Bill Gates Sr. had his son and Warren Buffett at dinner, and he went around the table asking each person to name the single thing that had mattered most to their success. He gave them paper. Gates and Buffett wrote independently.

Both wrote the same word. Focus.

Now, most people read that and nod. Of course, focus, work hard on one thing. And they miss what focus actually cost these two men.

Buffett could have done almost anything. He understood businesses better than nearly anyone alive. He turned down thousands of deals he was fully capable of making money on. Gates could have taken Microsoft into a dozen industries. He said no to most of them.

That is the part nobody wants to hear. Focus is not choosing what to do. Choosing what to do is easy and it feels wonderful. Focus is saying no to a hundred good things, things you could genuinely succeed at, so that one thing gets everything you have.

I see this constantly with promoters. The failing ones are almost never short of opportunities. They have too many. A new vertical, a second brand, a tempting partnership, a plot of land someone offered cheap. Each one is a good idea on its own. Together they are the reason the core business never becomes great.

The discipline is not spotting the opportunity. Anyone can spot it. The discipline is looking at a genuinely good opportunity, one you could win, and walking away from it because it is not the thing.

Strategy is not the long list of what you will do. It is the short, painful list of what you will not.
