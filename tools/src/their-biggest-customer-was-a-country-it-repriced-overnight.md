One year ago this month, America put a 50 percent tariff on Indian goods.

In Tirupur, the town that makes over half of India's knitwear exports, the phones went quiet. US orders crashed 60 to 70 percent. Around 3,000 crore of orders froze. Workers were sent home.

Understand why nobody could talk their way out. These exporters earn 8 to 15 percent margin. The new tax was 50 percent. You cannot discount your way across that gap. And rival countries were paying only 20 percent, so buyers simply looked elsewhere.

The units that depended mostly on America had no move left. Their real mistake was not made that August. It was made years earlier, when they let one country become most of their business.

Then came the second shock. Units that rushed to find new markets found every other unit rushing with them. Same pie, more hands. The exporters who came through comfortably were the ones already selling to Europe, the UK and Indian retail years before the trouble. They had built their exits early.

One year on, the town has survived. Not one factory shut, says their association. Their own words on the year: this episode has been a lesson.

Here is that lesson, and it is not about exports.

A country is a customer. A platform is a customer. Your one big client is a customer. If any one of them can cut your sales in half with one decision made in a room you will never enter, you are not running a strategy. You are running a dependency that is having a good year.

So do this one thing this month. Take your sales sheet. Find the single biggest dependency, one client, one market, one channel. If it is above 30 percent, start building the second door now. One new market. One new channel. One new large customer. It will feel slow and unnecessary, exactly like insurance does.

Because the second door costs very little when things are good. During the fire, it is not available at any price.
