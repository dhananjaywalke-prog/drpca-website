A promoter asked me how he could grow his profit. New markets, new products, maybe an acquisition.

I asked him a smaller question first. Which of your products makes you the most money.

He said all of them do. The company is profitable.

That is the most common answer I hear, and it is not an answer. Overall profit tells you the business survives. It does not tell you which parts are carrying the rest.

So we did three weeks of unglamorous work. Every cost, salaries, freight, power, interest, discounts, credit period, pushed down honestly to each product and each customer. No new software. His own data, and a lot of patience.

The picture surprised him.

His largest customer, the one everyone in the office was proud of, was barely breaking even once the volume discounts, the free delivery and the ninety day credit were counted. Two small product lines nobody talked about were earning most of the margin in the company.

He did not need a new market. He needed to know his own numbers.

Now the part worth thinking about. That exercise was always available to him. No permission needed, no capital, no consultant. It had been sitting there for eleven years.

Why did he avoid it. Because it is boring. Because there is nothing to announce when you finish. Because nobody was chasing him. And quietly, because it might say something uncomfortable about a customer he had built the factory around.

That is the pattern in almost every business I have seen. The highest return work is rarely the most difficult work. It is the tedious work with no deadline and no audience, which is exactly why it is still undone, and exactly why the value is still sitting in it.

So pick one. The thing you have postponed for over a year, that nobody will ever remind you about. Give it one hour this week. Just the first hour.

Opportunity does not usually hide where it is hard to reach. It hides where it is boring to look.
